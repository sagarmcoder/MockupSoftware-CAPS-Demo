# Mock Interview

Do not open solutions. Work one stage at a time and narrate your decisions.

## Stage 1 — Explore

Take a few minutes to familiarize yourself with the project. Tell the interviewer what you inspect and what you think each process does.

## Stage 2 — UI modification

Make the employee summary panel slightly wider. Verify the result in the browser.

## Stage 3 — Small React task

Add a configurable subtitle to the employee summary card and pass it from its parent.

## Stage 4 — SQL exploration

Find where time entries and payroll periods are stored. Describe the relevant relationships.

## Stage 5 — Basic query

Show all approved time entries from August 2026.

## Stage 6 — Grouping

Show total approved hours for each employee, keeping employees with zero hours.

## Stage 7 — Payroll and dates

Show total hours worked during the pay period containing August 18, 2026.

## Stage 8 — Join

Add department information to that result.

## Stage 9 — Debugging

The payroll total becomes larger after joining project assignments. Explain and correct it.

## Stage 10 — APPLY

Return each employee and their most recent time entry, keeping employees with none. Choose an appropriate technique and justify it.

## Stage 11 — Full stack

Add Client Name to the time-entry screen by tracing database → API → JSON → React.

## Stage 12 — Explain

Walk us through why you implemented the full-stack change that way, what you tested, and any assumptions you made.

## Interviewer prompts to expect

- Before writing code, tell me how you would approach this.
- What if we used the existing function instead?
- Could you solve this without that subquery?
- Take another look at what happens to employees without matching rows.
- What datatype are you actually comparing there?
- Show me how you know the browser received the value.

## Self-review after the session

Did you inspect before editing, explain your plan, ask useful questions, accept hints, verify assumptions, test changes, and communicate uncertainty honestly?
